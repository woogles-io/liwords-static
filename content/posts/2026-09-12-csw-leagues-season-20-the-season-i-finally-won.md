**Prologue**

Going into Season 20, I wasn't expecting anything particularly special. Other than a quick look at the 6-vowel 9s, I'd barely done any word study leading up to the season. Plus, I had a rather mediocre result in Season 19, finishing 9th with a 7-7 -185 record, just barely scraping by. 

However, this season, something changed. Somehow, everything was going my way.

This is the season I finally won.

<br><br>

<br>

**Exhibit B - Not Good Enough**

In this position, I'm up 64 against Xadreco, holding this rack.

Thinking I've pretty much iced this game, I played H1 AVI(S)O for 24 without much thought. While this play still wins about 95.9% according to Macondo, it actually was a medium-sized mistake.

My best guess as to why AVISO is bad lies in what Xadreco could do after AVISO. For example, a worst-case 6-tile overlap bingo to ANTIENT# would easily score over 90, and if my draw after AVISO is clunky, I could still lose the game. Another possibility is that Xadreco could have a hard-hitting bingo ending in A, such as 1A HEKETAR(A)#. 

Here, Macondo prefers I3 E(N)VOI for 18, which attempts to block some possible bingoes, while also reducing Xadreco's bingo scores. Another hidden benefit of ENVOI lies in its sneaky front R hook for RENVOI. By keeping the R, I get first dibs at using the spot if Xadreco were to bingo.

Unfortunately, just like in the jellomochas game, ENVOI would have won given that EHJRK were the actual tiles in the bag (drawn in that order). Even with his flexible rack of ACEERT?, Xadreco's bingoes wouldn't score enough to outrun H1 JAR(S) for 42, reaping the benefits of the aforementioned R(ENVOI) hook.

![](/images/uploads/image38.png)

In the actual game, after my EHJR draw, Xadreco played the crushing CREATEs for 100, while I was forced to play (C)HEER for 42. After his outplay, catching me with the high-scoring J, I wound up losing by 16.

![](/images/uploads/image16.jpg)

What I found most unfortunate was that I had played almost perfectly prior to AVISO, making only two small mistakes. While I had a way lower MI than Xadreco, this one seemingly small mistake effectively cost me this game.

**Hope Scrabble**

In this position, I'm down 81 against MGI (Lukeman Owolabi) and running out of time.

![](/images/uploads/image50.jpg)

There are no good bingo lanes left other than the C and U of CUB. Desperately needing a bingo to hold on, I tried looking for any fish that could have even the slightest chance of winning, with AEIIIIMOR unseen. After some looking, I saw that if I dump an E and draw an A out of the bag, I would threaten 3F (U)NREASON for 70.

So, I played 12C (SAX)E for 11, and miraculously, I drew the A. Macondo initially approves of my choice here. However, the problem is that it's using simulation here, meaning it assumes that Lukeman will make his top equity plays that shed his I's and don't score much while leaving UNREASON open, something a player of Lukeman's caliber wouldn't do. 

In fact, after running the pre-endgame solver, it determines that there are no winning pathways in this position, with the best play for minimizing spread being B1 EON. 

Even if Lukeman doesn't block the U and I draw the A, (SAX)E# still loses as Lukeman will play either 9G ROM or REM if he has the M, or 9G REO if he doesn't, simply outscoring my bingo.

![](/images/uploads/image48.jpg)

Still, if I were in a similar position in the future, I may still go with the same strategy, essentially playing hope chess and hoping they'd screw up. I'd definitely consider trying fishes like this against weaker opponents who are less likely to detect threats like this.

In the actual game, Lukeman wisely blocked UNREASON with 3F (U)M, securing him the win.

![](/images/uploads/image14.jpg)

**MI Atrocity**

I'm in a tough position this turn against tonyleah, down 1 point and holding this rack.

![](/images/uploads/image25.jpg)

Unsure of what to do here, I ended up playing the rather braindead K4 DYE for 18, keeping the rather awful CGIX. This was a large mistake according to Macondo, which preferred I11 (TO)GE# for 6, cleverly setting up the X on my rack. 

On the very next turn, I was faced with yet another tough position.

![](/images/uploads/image37.jpg)

Here, I made the genuinely awful play of 3L (H)EX for 26, reducing my win rate by a whopping 21.6%. Not only had I completely whiffed the nice disconnected play of D7 EXC(ID)E(D) for 52, I had also once again missed the possibility of setting up my X, this time with I11 (TO)G, setting up the sneaky EXCITE hooking TOGE# for the next turn. 

Heading into the pre-endgame, I'm leading by 2 holding this rack.

![](/images/uploads/image5-1.jpg)

I very quickly saw my bingo here: E4 NICTATE for 72. Instead, I chose to play 4K (DI)T for 6.

I correctly chose to pass up the bingo, as looking at the unseen (ACEFINST?), it is very likely that Tony would have winning out-bingoes in response. When I was actually going through the possible draws though, I realised that I don't really hit many bingoes in multiple spots. The N gives me 2E ENACTING and 14I ANCIENT, and the blank would also give those two plus many more. However, every other draw either yields nothing or only bingoes in 1 spot. After some thought though, I couldn't really think of anything else. Thus, I just went with it, hoping for the best.

While Macondo really likes (DI)T, the problem is that once again, it's assuming that Tony will make his top equity play rather than plays that make sense strategically in this position. In fact, after running the pre-endgame solver, it concludes that 2F CATTIN(G) is best, blocking as many of the bingo lines as possible, and gunning for a quick out next turn holding the flexible E, losing whenever Tony either bingoes or has something high-scoring overlapping CATTING. 

![](/images/uploads/image20.png)

Meanwhile, after running the pre-endgame solver on (DI)T, it concludes that it only wins whenever I draw a bingo in two spots (aforementioned N & ? draws), while Tony has no playable bingoes.

![](/images/uploads/image15.png)

In the actual game, I miraculously drew the blank, meaning my rather significant mistake here went unpunished. After Tony's I14 ACETIN, his best play, I went out with zINCATE for 70 to win by 55.

![](/images/uploads/image8-1.jpg)

This was unfortunately my worst-played game this season, with an awful 5.4 MI. Missing the X setup twice along with other strategical mistakes essentially doomed my MI.

In fact, in this position I had the very savvy E9 I(N)ROAD for 35, winning 9.0% more often than RORT#, my third straight large mistake.

And believe it or not, we're not done here! I missed another play in the same spot next turn, compounding a total of 4 MI in just 4 turns. 

![](/images/uploads/image4-1.jpg)

After DIKA#, I'm still down by 7, which was quickly made larger with shafiquet's play of 9H (S)I(N)E. 

![](/images/uploads/image47.jpg)

However, this was a huge break for me. Somehow, SINE loses them a mammoth 70.4% win percentage.

<br>

![](/images/uploads/image39.jpg)

Given the unseen (shafiquet's POV) of ACINOOTYY, one possible threat for him to deal with is the Y scoring spot on 11E, where plays like 11A COO(E)Y could prove devastating in such a close game. As you can see from the image above, Macondo's top play here is initially 12D (M)I for 4, suggesting it wins 100% of the time. 

However, upon deeper analysis with the pre-endgame solver, it determines that the best play is actually a sneaky fit of 9G EO(S)I(N) for 29. 

Honestly, I'm still not too sure why EOSIN is so strong here. My best guess is that this play not only scores a lot, but also leaves the K8 square open, allowing him to slot his S there for another 26 points in the endgame. 

In fact, with NY being in the bag, EO(S)I(N) would've been a winning play. My best sequence after EO(S)I(N) is A(Z)OTI(C) followed by C(O)O(E)Y, which loses by 1.

![](/images/uploads/image27.png)

(M)I, on the other hand, only wins 48.61% of the time, a far cry from the initial 100%. From what I understand, I would oftentimes be able to play something on row 7 like 7C TOYO or 7B COATI, or something on row 12 like B2 CO(MI)TY or G12 YIN. All of these plays, depending on what I draw, prevent shafiquet from playing EO(S)I(N) or EO(S)I(N)E in the endgame. The row 7 plays also draw outplays next turn.  

So while (S)I(N)E doesn't lose nearly as much win percentage as 70.4%, it is still a sizable mistake, and a big break for me.

![](/images/uploads/image45.png)

In the actual game, my endgame rack was ACIOOTY, and I immediately put down 11A COO(E)Y for 43, securing me the win. 

While I by no means played well in this game, especially with my 2nd highest MI this season of 4.9, I benefitted from shafiquet's huge late-game blunder, stealing a probably undeserved win.

It was starting to feel like this pattern of getting away with awful mistakes would end up defining my entire season.

**Part 2: The Race for First**

Three days into the season, kfraley was leading with an 8-3 +750 record. I was right behind him with my 7-3 +289 record, followed by jellomochas (6-2 +240) in third. In order to win the season, I needed to win my remaining 4 games, while both kfraley and jellomochas had to lose 1 or more games. If both kfraley and I won our remaining games, he'd likely leapfrog me in the standings as he has a way better spread tiebreaker. As I was leading in all my remaining games, all I could do now was pray that both of them would lose games.

The next day, I completed two more games and brought my record to 9-3 +493, temporarily overtaking kfraley. While jellomochas had lost a game, bringing his record to 6-3 +136, kfraley had still not completed a game yet.

![](/images/uploads/image22.jpg)

At this point, it was really just the three of us with three losses. All that mattered now is who could sustain it. 

Since my game against ather was practically over (100+ pt lead), my game against gharp started to feel like a “decider”, almost. Even if kfraley and jellomochas lost another game after this, if I lost to gharp, kfraley would once again have more than enough spread to overtake me. 
<br>

After he bingoes with 13A CRISTAE for 79, I do indeed get down DORADOS to stay one step ahead, for now.

In this position, the 3 plays I considered were 11C J(E)E for 20, 14J JONE(S) for 28, and H1 E(R)GON# for 21. And somehow, I once again managed to pick the worst of the three options: ERGON#.

If I had played JEE or JONES, I'd have kept a far superior leave, so even if gharp bingoed or played a high scoring 5, my odds wouldn't be too bad for drawing a game-winning bingo. **Afterword**

I never expected myself to be writing this.

When I first joined the CSW Leagues in Season 2, I started in Division 6, paired with strong players like merlion (Tony Sim), tonyleah (obvious), and RonanD10 (Ronan Dance). At that time, I was nowhere near as strong of a player as I am now, and my “goal” was simply to survive the division. Instead, I went 11-3 +742, finishing 2nd just behind merlion. I couldn't tell you how lucky I was. Out of the 5 people in the promotion zone, my MiAV was the worst at 4.3, and I averaged less than 1.5 bingoes per game! I was carried to Division 5 by sheer, undeserved luck.

Going into subsequent seasons, my goal had shifted slightly. Now that I'd proven that I could survive, I wanted to see how far up I could climb. I got all the way to Division 3 in Season 5, before finally hitting a roadblock. I was quite content, though. I never thought I'd be good enough to compete with the absolute best anyway. It truly felt like I had found the division I belonged in. However, 2 seasons later, I broke free, and by Season 9, I was in Division 1.

At this point, my MiAV had stabilised at somewhere around low 3s to high 2s, and my goal shifted once again: don't relegate. When I was relegated in Season 10, I wasn't particularly upset. I wasn't supposed to be in Division 1, anyway.  

So when I re-promoted in Season 14, my goal stayed the same: survive. Instead, I finished 11th, which normally meant relegation. But once again, through some absurd luck, someone in Division 2 just so happened to drop out. Against all odds, I had survived Division 1 yet again.

Over the next few seasons, things started to change. In Season 16, I finished 6th with a 9-5 +87 record. In Season 17, I got even closer - 3rd with an 8-6 +227 record. In Season 18, I came 2 blown endgames away from winning, finishing 7-7 +313, but, more importantly, my best-ever MiAV at 2.4. That was when I realised that perhaps, I _did_ have a chance at winning this. 

I did well not to bring my hopes up too high though, and after my Season 19 performance, I figured my transient little hot streak was over.

I guess not.
